The global education technology market was valued at approximately 142 billion USD in 2023 and is projected to reach 348 billion USD by 2030, growing at a compound annual growth rate of around 13.6 percent. However, this market requires careful segmentation because the post-pandemic period has produced starkly different outcomes for different EdTech categories. Enterprise learning and development software, higher education infrastructure, and K-12 institutional tools have shown durable growth, while consumer-facing self-improvement and test preparation apps experienced significant contraction after 2021.

The pandemic created an artificial peak in EdTech adoption between 2020 and 2021, when global school closures forced every educational institution to adopt digital platforms. Global EdTech funding hit a record 21 billion USD in 2021. By 2023, annual global EdTech funding had contracted to approximately 5.6 billion USD, reflecting both macroeconomic conditions and investor skepticism following high-profile collapses such as Byju's, which went from a peak valuation of 22 billion USD to severe financial distress by 2024.

The K-12 segment faces distinct challenges compared to higher education and corporate learning. Consumer retention is the central problem. Studies consistently show that voluntary self-paced online courses have completion rates of 5 to 15 percent, and subscription-based K-12 tutoring apps see monthly churn rates of 8 to 15 percent once schools reopen for in-person instruction. The most successful K-12 EdTech companies are those that sell to schools as institutions rather than to parents, because institutional contracts provide stable recurring revenue and align with how teaching actually happens.

Higher education EdTech has performed more consistently. Online program management companies that partner with universities to deliver accredited degrees online have achieved strong unit economics, with companies like 2U, Coursera for Campus, and Emeritus building significant scale. The primary driver is the credential value that institutions confer on digital degrees, which self-paced platforms cannot replicate. Completion rates for degree-linked online programs average 50 to 70 percent, compared to sub-15 percent for non-credentialed MOOC courses.

Corporate learning and development software is the most financially resilient EdTech segment. Enterprises spend approximately 350 billion USD per year on employee training globally. Platforms like Cornerstone OnDemand, Degreed, 360Learning, and LinkedIn Learning target this market. B2B EdTech companies benefit from multi-year enterprise contracts, low churn once integrated with HR systems, and ability to demonstrate ROI through productivity metrics. Net revenue retention in best-in-class enterprise EdTech platforms exceeds 120 percent.

India's EdTech market presents a cautionary tale. India was the world's largest EdTech market by number of users during the pandemic, with Byju's, Unacademy, Vedantu, and upGrad collectively valued at over 30 billion USD at their peak. By 2023 and 2024, the sector had undergone severe rationalization. Byju's faced insolvency proceedings, Unacademy reduced headcount by over 60 percent, and Vedantu pivoted from consumer to institutional markets. The core problem was unsustainable customer acquisition costs driven by aggressive sales tactics, high churn rates, and the return of offline coaching as the dominant mode after pandemic restrictions lifted.

The segments within India EdTech that have shown sustainable economics include B2B school management software, language learning for professional upskilling, coding education for employment placement, and government-partnership edtech serving rural students. The National Education Policy of 2020 created tailwinds for digital learning infrastructure at the institutional level. Skilling platforms that can demonstrate employment outcomes for their graduates command premium pricing and lower churn.

Key metrics that differentiate sustainable EdTech businesses from unsustainable ones include course completion rate above 40 percent for the core product, monthly active user retention exceeding 60 percent at 90 days, net promoter scores above 50, student outcome metrics such as exam scores or employment rates, and customer acquisition costs recovered within 12 months through subscription or repeat purchase revenue. EdTech companies that cannot demonstrate learning efficacy face increasing scrutiny from investors and regulators alike.
