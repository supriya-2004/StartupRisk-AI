Global e-commerce retail sales reached approximately 5.8 trillion USD in 2023 and are projected to exceed 8 trillion USD by 2027. E-commerce now accounts for roughly 19 percent of all retail sales globally, with significant variation by country. China leads with approximately 46 percent of retail happening online, followed by South Korea at 35 percent, the United Kingdom at 27 percent, and the United States at approximately 22 percent. India's e-commerce penetration remains at 7 to 9 percent of total retail, representing significant runway for growth given a total retail market exceeding 800 billion USD.

The India e-commerce market was valued at approximately 70 billion USD in 2023 and is forecast to reach 300 billion USD by 2030. The market is dominated by Flipkart, owned by Walmart, and Amazon India, with Meesho emerging strongly in the value segment targeting tier-2 and tier-3 cities. The largest product categories are mobile phones and electronics, fashion and apparel, home and furniture, beauty and personal care, and grocery. The grocery category is growing the fastest, driven by the rapid expansion of quick commerce platforms.

Quick commerce, defined as grocery and essentials delivery within 10 to 30 minutes, has been the most significant structural shift in India e-commerce since 2021. Zomato's Blinkit, Swiggy Instamart, and Zepto have collectively reached over 60 cities and processed an estimated 3 million daily orders by 2024. The quick commerce model requires dense dark store networks, significant upfront capital, and geographic concentration. Gross margins in quick commerce are typically negative at the unit level during expansion, relying on scale and category mix improvement to achieve profitability. Zepto raised capital at a 1.4 billion USD valuation in 2024, demonstrating investor conviction despite near-term losses.

Direct-to-consumer brands, known as D2C brands, have been one of the dominant startup themes in India e-commerce since 2018. D2C brands bypass traditional retail distribution by selling directly through their own websites and apps, supplemented by marketplace presence. The advantage is higher gross margins compared to selling through traditional retail channels and direct customer relationships that enable data-driven product development. Successful India D2C brands include Mamaearth in personal care, Licious in meat delivery, Wakefit in furniture, and Boat in consumer electronics. However, D2C brand building requires sustained marketing investment, and rising digital advertising costs on Facebook and Google have compressed D2C unit economics significantly since 2021.

Unit economics represent the central challenge for e-commerce startups. Contribution margin, which measures revenue minus variable costs including product cost, logistics, payment processing, and customer returns, must be positive on a per-order basis for a business to be financially viable. Indian e-commerce logistics costs average between 55 and 100 rupees per shipment for forward delivery, with reverse logistics for returns costing 70 to 150 rupees per unit. Fashion and apparel categories face return rates of 20 to 35 percent, substantially eroding contribution margins. Electronics face lower return rates but higher logistics costs due to weight and fragility.

Customer acquisition cost in e-commerce has risen sharply. Performance marketing CPCs on Google and Facebook in India have increased by 40 to 60 percent between 2020 and 2023. Marketplaces charge commission rates of 15 to 30 percent of selling price depending on category. The best e-commerce brands achieve payback on customer acquisition within 2 to 3 purchases, relying on repeat purchase rates to justify initial CAC. Subscription models, loyalty programs, and bundled product categories are common strategies to increase purchase frequency and extend customer lifetime value.

Social commerce is an emerging channel that is particularly relevant in India, where WhatsApp has over 500 million users and Instagram has over 200 million. Meesho built its early growth by enabling resellers to share product links in WhatsApp groups, effectively turning its customer base into a distributed sales force. Live commerce, popular in China, is being piloted by several India e-commerce platforms and may represent a significant channel shift over the next 3 to 5 years.

Key risks for e-commerce startups include inventory management and working capital requirements, dependency on third-party logistics providers, counterfeit product risks on marketplace platforms, platform risk from Amazon and Flipkart fee changes, and regulatory uncertainty around FDI rules governing e-commerce that have been tightened multiple times since 2018. Businesses selling regulated categories such as food, pharmaceuticals, or financial products face additional compliance requirements including FSSAI licensing, drug licensing, and NBFC registration respectively.
